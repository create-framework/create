# Geometry

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Shape` / `Region` / `Curve` | The geometry's traits, so every shape of a kind answers the same methods and generic code takes any (`def f[C: Curve](c: C)`). `Shape(Drawable)`, every one of the eleven, `Copyable` and `Deinitable`: `bounds`, `closest_point`, `translate`. `Region(Shape)`, the seven with an inside (`Rectangle`, `Circle`, `Ellipse`, `Capsule`, `Triangle`, `Sector`, `Polygon`), which `canvas.clip` takes: `center`, `area`, `contains(Point2D)`, `move_to`. `Curve(Shape)`, the four without (`Line`, `Arc`, `Bezier`, `Spline` — a straight line is a curve, as in mathematics): `at`, `tangent` (the derivative, not unit), `length`, `at_distance`, `flatten`, `beziers`. Tests between two shapes (`overlaps`, `contains(shape)`, `intersects`) stay overloads per pair, so they are not trait methods; nor is a curve's `move_to`, which only `Line` (its midpoint) and `Arc` (its centre) have, or `split(t)`, which `Line`, `Arc` and `Bezier` have — a piece of a `Spline` is not a `Spline` |
| `overlaps` / `intersects` / `contains` | `overlaps(a, b)`: free, symmetric, regions only (`Rectangle`/`Circle`/`Ellipse`/`Capsule`/`Triangle`/`Sector`/`Polygon`). `curve.intersects(x)`: every curve (`Line`, `Arc`, `Bezier`, `Spline`) against every shape and `Point2D`, both ways round, since a curve has no interior. `region.contains(x)`: asymmetric, every region against every region and every curve. A curve is never a region. All three see a shape's `corner_radius`. `Line` and `Arc` are exact (to `_RADIUS_EPSILON` with an arc); `Bezier` and `Spline` flatten themselves to within 1e-4 of their size, so a crossing is always found but a graze or near miss is decided only that closely; a region's `contains` follows `Arc`, `Bezier` and `Spline` the same way |
| `corner_radius` | A keyword-only field of `Rectangle`, `Triangle`, `Polygon` and `Sector` (`Rectangle(p, w, h, corner_radius=8)`): a shape value is drawn and clipped by its own radius, whatever the style says. The scalar canvas calls (`rectangle`, `triangle`, `polygon`, `sector`) take a `corner_radius=` keyword, and without one use the style's: `canvas.corner_radius(r)`, `Style(corner_radius=)`, `canvas.style(corner_radius=)`. A named keyword beats the style, `corner_radius=0` included. Clamped as drawn: a rectangle to half its shorter side, a triangle's one radius so no corner overruns half an edge; a polygon's and a sector's corners each on their own, a concave corner filling its notch. Every `contains`/`overlaps`/`intersects`/`closest_point`/`area` sees it: rectangles and triangles exactly, polygons and sectors through their stroke with each curve flattened (within ~0.1% of the radius). A rounded polygon or sector draws as a polygon of more vertices, so it costs more stroked (see `Polygon`) |
| `Rectangle` | Centre-positioned, `w` by `h`, upright unless turned: `rotation=` (keyword-only, radians counter-clockwise about `position`, default 0) on `Rectangle` and `canvas.rectangle`. Every test sees the turned shape exactly; `bounds()` and `left()`/`right()`/`bottom()`/`top()` are its upright bounding box's. Drawn as the upright rectangle under a rotation, so rounding, stroke and a gradient turn with it and a shadow's offset stays screen-fixed, as under `canvas.transform` |
| `Ellipse` | Axis-aligned, a region: `position` its centre and `w`/`h` its full width and height, as a `Rectangle`'s, whose `bounds()` it fills (`canvas.ellipse(p, w, h)`, Processing's `ellipse`); turn one with `canvas.transform`. Tests against a point, `Line`, `Circle`, another `Ellipse` and a sharp `Rectangle`/`Triangle`/`Polygon` are exact — the stretch onto the unit circle keeps those kinds, and a circle or ellipse goes through the exact closest point on an ellipse; against a `Sector` or an `Arc` the ellipse stands in as its outline polygon, within 1e-4 of its size. A zero `w` or `h` is its zero-size bounds, in every test and when drawn. Drawn as a polygon flattened as finely as a circle of its larger radius, so its stroke is as thick all round and placed by `stroke_alignment`; filled, it costs what a circle does, stroked about three times as much; no inset shadow |
| `Capsule` | Every point within `r` of the segment `start`→`end`, a region: the round collider for a moving body, at any angle (`canvas.capsule(start, end, r)`). Placed by its ends like a `Line`, so `center()` and `move_to` are the midpoint's; `start == end` is the `Circle` there, and a stadium is already `Rectangle(p, w, h, corner_radius=h / 2, rotation=a)`. Every test is a distance from the segment, exact against everything — points, lines, arcs, circles, ellipses, capsules, straight-edged shapes and rounded rectangles and triangles — except holding a `Sector`'s arc or an `Ellipse`, which it follows within 1e-4. A zero `r` is the segment, and draws nothing. Drawn as a polygon, each end flattened as finely as a circle of `r`, its gradient spanning its bounds; filled it costs what a circle does, stroked about 2.3 times as much; no inset shadow |
| `Polygon` | Any `vertices`, in order, closed back to the first: concave or crossing itself, a region. Inside by the **nonzero rule** (a pentagram of five crossing vertices is solid through its middle), vertex order irrelevant, boundary inside. `Polygon.regular(position, r, sides)` and `Polygon.star(position, r, inner_radius, tips)` start at `start_angle`, straight up by default. `center()` is the area centroid and `area()` the shoelace area, both meaningful only for a simple polygon. `canvas.polygon` places its stroke along the outside by `stroke_alignment` but centres it on an edge running through the inside (a pentagram's inner pentagon), so every edge is stroked as thick; no inset shadow. `corner_radius` rounds every vertex (not the crossings of a self-crossing one). Stroked, on the CPU at `MEDIUM`, a 5-tip star costs ~160 µs a frame, a pentagram ~380 µs, a 40-tip star ~2 ms; rounded, ~360 µs, ~460 µs and ~20 ms. Filled only, a fraction of that |
| Sweep | `Arc` and `Sector` share `position` (the circle's centre), `r`, `start_angle` and a **signed** `sweep_angle`, in radians from +x: positive is counter-clockwise (y is up), negative clockwise, and \|sweep\| ≥ `TAU` is the whole circle. Stored as given, so `==` and printing round-trip; `end_angle()` is `start_angle + sweep_angle` |
| `Sector` | A pie slice, a region: its tip at `position`, fanned out to its arc. Wider than a half turn it is not convex — the missing wedge is outside it, for `contains` and `overlaps` alike. `center()` is `position`, like `Circle`'s, not the centroid. `canvas.sector` places its stroke by `stroke_alignment`, as a circle's; no inset shadow. `corner_radius` rounds the tip and both ends of the arc, the ends tangent to the arc from inside; past a half turn the tip is concave and its rounding fills the notch, at exactly a half turn it runs straight and stays, and a whole disc has no corners |
| `Arc` | A curve, like `Line`: no interior, never in `overlaps`. Constant speed, so `at_distance(d)` is exact. `beziers()` splits it into Béziers of at most 45°; `canvas.arc` strokes those as one chain, like `canvas.spline` |
| `Bezier` | A cubic Bézier: `start`, `control1`, `control2`, `end`; passes through the endpoints only. A curve, not a region: `canvas.bezier` strokes it in the stroke style and draws nothing with the stroke off. `at(t)` is uneven in speed; `at_distance(d)` moves along it at constant speed. `closest_point` is a search (samples, then Newton), accurate to rounding |
| `Spline` | A smooth curve through every one of `points`, in order (Catmull-Rom); `closed` runs it on back to the first. `alpha` spaces its knots: 0 uniform (can loop where points bunch), 0.5 centripetal (default, never does), 1 chordal. Same sampling methods as `Bezier`, `t` split evenly per stretch. `beziers()` converts it exactly; `canvas.spline` draws it as one stroke of those Béziers |
